## 3. Cross-Cutting Analysis

### 3.1 Concepts With Multiple Models

- `Project` appears in at least two forms: `FurnitureProject` and `CabinetProject`.
- `Cabinet` appears as both `CabinetProject` and multiple manufacturing records/reports.
- `Panel` appears as `SceneNode`, `PanelSpec`, `NodeRole`, and inventory records.
- `Hardware` appears across registry, BOM, placement, usage, and cost reports.
- `Joinery` appears as an enum, a graph, and multiple intelligence/cost builders.
- `Material` appears across manufacturing specs, catalogs, cost calculators, and availability reports.
- `Quotation / Commercial Output` is composed from multiple DTOs and builders, not one single model.

### 3.2 Concepts Missing Explicit Models

- `Divider` does not appear to have a dedicated business aggregate or report; it is represented through `NodeRole.DIVIDER`, topology operations, and cabinet builder methods.
- `Project` geometry concepts are intentionally spread across ADRs and boundary contracts, but there is no single canonical `ProjectGeometry` model class in the codebase.
- `Manufacturing Report` is explicit as a family of reports, but not as one canonical report object that all other reports derive from.
- `Cost Report` is also a family of reports rather than a single canonical cost model.

### 3.3 Concepts Mixed Across Layers

- `FurnitureProjectBusinessReportBuilder` mixes manufacturing, cost, and commercial outputs in one pipeline.
- `FurnitureProjectQuotationBuilder` mixes manufacturing packaging with quotation generation.
- `ManufacturingRuntimePipelineBuilder` mixes extraction, adaptation, packaging, and production-package creation.
- `Hardware` spans domain, manufacturing, and cost modules.
- `Material` spans manufacturing and cost modules.
- `Joinery` spans domain, manufacturing, and cost modules.
- `Optimization / Nesting` is implemented in cost intelligence even though it is clearly tied to manufacturing efficiency.

### 3.4 Concepts That Appear Commercially Valuable

- `Hardware`
- `Material`
- `Edge Banding`
- `CNC Operation`
- `Manufacturing Report`
- `Cost Report`
- `Quotation / Commercial Output`
- `Optimization / Nesting`
- `Door`, `Drawer`, and `Shelf` because they feed quality, cost, and manufacturing decisions

### 3.5 Concepts That Appear Low-Value or Unclear

- `FactoryResourceCatalogService` and `FactoryGovernance*` modules are operationally relevant, but they are not clearly furniture-domain concepts.
- `CommercialAcceptanceContract` is commercially meaningful, but it reads more like process state than a furniture business entity.
- `SceneNode` is valuable, but it is a geometric DTO rather than a business model.
- `ManufacturingExecutiveReport` is an output DTO with limited domain semantics compared with the more specific manufacturing sub-reports.

## 4. Findings, Risks, Questions, Recommendation

### Findings

- The codebase contains a broad and commercially meaningful furniture/manufacturing vocabulary.
- `Project`, `Cabinet`, `Panel`, `Hardware`, `Joinery`, `Material`, and `Quotation` are all present, but not all as single canonical entities.
- The most complete inventories appear around hardware, manufacturing reports, and commercial output.
- Several business concepts are represented by multiple models across multiple layers, which suggests the repository currently prefers pipelines and DTO composition over a single domain aggregate per concept.

### Risks

- Concept drift is possible where the same business term maps to multiple models with slightly different meanings.
- Downstream cost and commercial pipelines may continue to accumulate implicit assumptions about manufacturing and geometry inputs.
- A few concept areas, especially `Divider` and `Project`, lack a single canonical model, which makes Phase 1 modeling work harder.

### Questions for Phase 1 Model Inventory

- Which `Project` model is canonical for business semantics: `FurnitureProject` or `CabinetProject`?
- Which `Panel` representation is authoritative for each downstream use case: `SceneNode`, `PanelSpec`, or a manufacturing record?
- Should `HardwareSpec` remain the canonical hardware model, or are there separate canonical models for BOM, cost, and placement?
